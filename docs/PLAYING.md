# Playing Red Horizon

## Game modes

| Mode | |
|---|---|
| **Skirmish** | Up to 3 AI opponents on five difficulties (*Recruit*, for your first battles, then Easy, Normal, Hard and Brutal), on generated or hand-made maps (temperate, winter or desert; land or sea). Any AI but one can be switched to your side (the handshake button on its row), for example a 2 vs 2 with an AI ally. Options: King of the Hill, and a build-up ceasefire of 5–30 minutes or unlimited, during which the AI builds up and only defends. |
| **Quick Battle** | One click from the main menu into a tuned random match at your level (see [Quick Battle](#quick-battle)). |
| **Multiplayer** | Up to four players through your own server: 1 vs 1, 2 vs 2, free for all, or co-op against the AI ([MULTIPLAYER.md](MULTIPLAYER.md)). **Surrender** is in the in-match menu (Esc) and counts as a loss: your side concedes, or, if a team-mate still plays, the AI takes over your forces. Skirmishes and ranked runs offer it too. If the server goes away mid-match, **Play on vs AI** finishes the battle on your machine. |
| **Campaigns** | 7 Allied and 7 Soviet missions with briefings, primary and optional objectives, radio transmissions and up to three stars each, on five difficulties (see [Campaign difficulty](#campaign-difficulty)). |
| **Boot Camp** | An interactive tutorial in nine drills. |
| **Special operations** | *Wolfpack*, *Iron Coast*, and Survival in three climates, alone or with AI teammates. |
| **World Conquest** | A Risk-style campaign on a 3D globe: start from any of 44 homelands and conquer the world, attacking with the dice or fighting the decisive battles yourself (see [World Conquest](#world-conquest)). |
| **Conquest** | A seeded roguelite campaign across Europe. Every territory is a real battle; veterans, research, perks and doctrines carry over between battles. |
| **Daily Challenge** | One seeded battle per day, with modifiers, a score and a streak. |
| **Challenges** | Turn any moment of a replay into a "can you win from here?" challenge file. |
| **Replays** | Every skirmish of 30 seconds or more is recorded. Watch at 1–8× speed from any player's view, or take command of any side at any moment and play on as a new branch (kept once you give it an order; the pause menu says when leaving would not keep a replay). |

## Skirmish rules: Commander or Classic

**Classic** is pure Red Alert. **Commander**, the default, adds:

- **Economy and tech evolution.** See [ECONOMY.md](ECONOMY.md) for the full guide.
  - The Construction Yard grows from tier 1 to 3.
  - Visible building upgrades: refinery processor, turbines, assembly line, armour shop,
    training ground, reinforced defences, satellite uplink.
  - Research at the Research Lab.
  - New structures: Mining Outpost, Ore Purifier or Smelter, Logistics Centre, garrisonable
    Bunker, Fortress Gate.
- **Doctrines.** Pick one before the match, for example *Armoured Fist*, *Human Wave*,
  *Blitzkrieg* or *Fortress Europe*. Each has two bonuses and one drawback, and more unlock as
  your rank rises.
- **Command points and powers.** Points come from combat and objectives, with a comeback bonus
  when you're behind. Spend them in a three-tier tree:
  - Recon Sweep, Field Repair, Emergency Funds;
  - Rally Cry, Elite Training, Artillery Barrage;
  - Reinforcements, Sabotage, and a faction ultimate: Tesla Storm or Aegis Shield.

  The AI uses them too.
- **Battlefield events** every few minutes: supply convoys to escort or ambush, airdrops, downed
  pilots, mercenaries and ore surges.
- **Neutral tech structures.** Oil derricks, field hospitals, radar outposts and communication
  arrays, captured with engineers.
- **Mutators.** 24 of them, such as *Golden Ore*, *Tank Rush*, *Lightning War* and *Night Ops*,
  or let *Surprise me* pick.
- **Dynamic difficulty** keeps matches close. It is on by default when every AI is Recruit or Easy:
  there it only eases off when you are struggling and never pushes harder. From Normal up it works
  both ways. Once you flip the switch, your choice is kept.
- **A beaten AI surrenders.** An AI left with no Construction Yard, no MCV and no factory it can pay for throws
  what army it has left at you, then surrenders, so you never have to hunt down its last helicopter or Ore Truck.
  It hunts down yours the same way: with nothing of yours in sight, its army splits up and searches the map.
- **AI allies on Recruit and Easy** play your support: they keep their army between your base and theirs, come
  to your base when it is attacked and join your attacks, but don't attack on their own.

## World Conquest

Pick a homeland on the globe (search by name or continent), a doctrine, a size and a difficulty:

- **Blitz:** your continent and its neighbours, about 30 territories, 2–3 hours. You win by holding every rival
  capital.
- **World:** about 100 territories, a long campaign. You win by eliminating every rival power.
- **Ironman:** losing your capital ends the campaign.

Every territory shows its troops. Each turn has three phases:

1. **Reinforce.** You get troops for every three territories you hold (at least 3), plus each bonus zone you hold
   whole, capitals, wonders and traded card sets. Select a territory, then *Place* or *Place all*.
2. **Attack.** From a territory with 2 or more troops into a neighbour (sea lanes need a port at one end). The odds
   panel shows the chance to take it and the expected losses before you commit. Then:
   - **Roll:** one roll of Risk's dice. You roll up to 3, the defender up to 2; the highest dice are compared pair
     by pair, and ties go to the defender.
   - **Blitz:** roll until the territory falls or you are down to 1 troop.
   - **Fight it (RTS):** play the battle on a map that looks like the region, with its landmark. The troops set the
     size of both forces, and the result comes back as losses on both sides. Fighting pays better than the dice:
     war chest, veterans, a chance at a card and more score.
3. **Fortify.** Move troops once between connected territories you hold, then end your turn.

Conquer at least one territory in a turn to earn a card; three of a kind, or one of each, trade for troops. The rival
powers play by the same rules, each with its own style; their turns play back quickly on the globe (*Skip* jumps to
your turn). The **Rivals** panel shows their troops, territories, cards and mood, and lets you offer a ceasefire;
breaking one costs you trust with everyone. When a rival attacks your capital or a wonder, you choose: let the dice
decide, or defend it yourself.

- **Weekly World:** the same world for everyone each week; your best score is kept on this device.
- **Challenge links:** *Copy challenge link* (setup and share screens) gives a link to the same world, homeland and
  difficulty, for a friend to beat your score.
- **Share card:** a picture of your empire on the globe with your stats, to download or share.
- **Keyboard and screen readers:** the **List view** (the list button, or <kbd>L</kbd>) lists every territory by
  zone; arrows move, <kbd>Enter</kbd> selects, just like tapping the globe. Arrow keys turn the globe, <kbd>+</kbd>
  and <kbd>−</kbd> zoom, <kbd>Esc</kbd> steps back.

## Quick Battle

- **Your level.** You start on Recruit. You move up one level once you have at least 3 wins at your
  current level and win at least 60% of your games there. If you have already done that on a higher
  level in regular skirmishes (say Hard), you go straight to the level above it.
- **Recruit and Easy** roll exactly one mutator, always a newcomer-safe one: a weather or night
  mutator, *Supply Drops*, *Veteran Corps*, *Heavy Metal* or *Big Guns*. Dynamic difficulty is on.
  From Normal up, Quick Battle rolls one or two mutators from its wider pool.
- **Rivals** never play above your level in Quick Battle, and at most one level above it in
  skirmish, so a Recruit-level commander meets at most an Easy rival.

## Campaign difficulty

The campaign briefing has a **Difficulty** picker: Recruit, Easy, Normal, Hard and Brutal. It applies to the Allied
and Soviet campaigns, *Wolfpack*, *Iron Coast* and custom missions. Survival and the Daily Challenge keep one level so
their leaderboards compare; co-op survival has its allies' own setting, and Conquest its run's difficulty.

- **How it works.** As in Red Alert's campaign, each level handicaps your side and the enemy's: firepower, rate of
  fire, speed, and build cost and time. The briefing lists the exact numbers for the level you pick.

  | Level | Your forces | The enemy |
  |---|---|---|
  | Recruit (Red Alert's Easy) | +20% firepower, +25% rate of fire, +20% speed, −20% build cost and time | −20% firepower, −17% rate of fire, −20% speed |
  | Easy | as usual | −20% firepower, −17% rate of fire, −20% speed |
  | Normal | as usual | as usual |
  | Hard | as usual | +20% firepower, +25% rate of fire, +20% speed, −20% build cost and time |
  | Brutal (Red Alert's Hard) | −20% firepower, −17% rate of fire, −20% speed | +20% firepower, +25% rate of fire, +20% speed, −20% build cost and time |

- **The missions adapt too.** On Recruit and Easy some missions field fewer defences and smaller raids, give you more
  time or send you reinforcements. On Hard and Brutal some send more against you, and where an enemy commander
  builds its own army it plays a level more cautiously, so the handicap above is what makes it hard. Brutal is meant
  for excellent players: some missions trim the enemy there so that it stays winnable.
- **When a mission is lost.** Besides its own conditions, a mission without a base is lost when nothing you have
  left could finish it (in *First Snow*, every heavy tank and the V2 gone while a gun still stands).
- **When to change it.** You can change the level between missions, or before a retry. A new commander starts on
  Easy, and until you pick a level the next mission starts at the level of your last win (Normal for a commander
  whose wins predate the picker). Your choice is remembered.
- **Records.** The briefing shows the hardest level you have won each mission at, the mission screen shows the level
  you are playing at, and the debrief shows it too. Stars, medals and unlocks don't depend on the level: every level
  completes the mission. Saved games keep their level.

## Progression

- **Ranks and medals.** XP, 15 ranks per faction, 52 medals (some hidden) and lifetime
  statistics.
- **Contracts.** 3 daily and 3 weekly goals that pay XP and Commendations. You get one free
  reroll a day, and missing a day costs nothing.
- **The Locker.** Cosmetics earned by playing: paint schemes, portraits, titles, banners, HUD
  themes, announcer voices and victory fanfares. Nothing is for sale, and nothing affects
  gameplay.
- **Results screen.** After every match it shows XP and contract progress, battle charts (army
  value, income, kills), your MVP unit, the highlights, the next unlock, and *Play again*
  (Enter) or *Rematch*. After a skirmish defeat or a close win, an *Economy* block compares your
  harvest and peak army with the strongest enemy's, with one suggestion (for example, build a
  second Ore Refinery, put up defences before the first attack, send tanks against the defences
  your infantry kept dying to, or attack-move instead of plain moves).
- **Field manual hints** (*Settings › Gameplay*) coach you during a skirmish. They tell you when an
  enemy earns far more than you, when you still have one Ore Refinery at 6:00, when your factories
  stand idle with money in the bank, when your power has been low for a while, when the first
  enemy attack is coming and you have no defences, when your last Ore Truck is gone, when your
  infantry keeps dying to enemy defences (tanks are the answer), when units die on plain move orders
  (use attack-move), when your attacks are beaten one small group at a time, and when a structure
  stays badly damaged. When money piles up ($1,500 in the bank for a minute) it points you to
  Alt-click looping (the loop button on touch screens), and once loops run, to a second War Factory or
  Barracks. When you are winning (the enemy has lost half its structures, or your army is worth twice
  its army and defences, from 8:00) it tells you to finish the job: select every combat unit and
  attack-move on the enemy's last structures. The radar pings the ones you have seen (never what the
  fog hides) and the alert's *Show* button takes the camera there; if you have seen none, it suggests
  scouting. Each shows at most once a match and for under a minute, never more than one a minute,
  and a click dismisses it; against Recruit and Easy opponents the money and close-out reminders may
  come back (up to three times, a few minutes apart) unless you dismiss them. Campaign missions get the
  battle and base-keeping ones (infantry against defences, attack-move, attacking together, power, idle
  factories, money in the bank, Ore Trucks, repairs), and only advice the mission lets you follow; the
  economy race, the close-out and the build-order steps are skirmish-only. A side you surrendered (or
  an AI runs) gets no coaching.

## Armies and mechanics

- **Factions.** The classic Allied and Soviet rosters:
  - infantry, from riflemen to Tanya, spies, thieves and attack dogs;
  - tanks, artillery and V2s;
  - aircraft;
  - a full navy;
  - the expansion units: Tesla Tank, M.A.D. Tank, Chrono Tank, Demolition Truck, Shock Trooper,
    Mechanic and Phase Transport.
- **Economy and power.**
  - Ore and gem fields regrow, and harvesters queue at refineries.
  - Silos store credits.
  - On low power, production slows and defences go dark.
- **Classic mechanics.**
  - MCV deployment and adjacency placement; selling and repairing.
  - Engineers capture buildings and rebuild bridges, and Tanya plants C4.
  - Tanks crush infantry, transports carry troops, and aircraft rearm on pads.
  - Vehicles sent to a service depot take turns on its pad and leave fully repaired.
  - Units busy with a structure return fire when something armed shoots them, then carry on.
  - Fog of war shows "ghosts" of enemy buildings as you last saw them.
- **Espionage and superweapons.**
  - Spies, gap generators, radar jammers, camouflaged pillboxes and decoys.
  - Support powers: GPS, sonar pulse, spy plane, paratroopers and parabombs.
  - The Iron Curtain, the Chronosphere and the nuclear missile.
- **Command tools.**
  - Formations, patrol, escort and guard; stances; queued waypoints.
  - Build plans, which the construction yard places in order.
  - Looping production, auto-groups, an army overview and one-key repair runs.
- **AI.**
  - It plays under the same fog of war as you, scouts and counters your army.
  - It raids, flanks and expands.
  - 11 commander personalities, and persistent rivals that remember how you play.

## Accessibility

- **Team colours:** *Default*, *Colour-blind friendly* (Okabe-Ito colours, safe for the common types of
  colour blindness), or *You vs enemies* (you blue, every enemy orange). This applies to the battlefield,
  minimap and HUD.
- **Health bars:** tick marks at 50% and 25%, so the colour isn't the only cue.
- **Interface size:** 80–150%.
- **Subtitles:** on by default, for announcer lines, with four sizes. Mission and rival radio is also
  read out to screen readers.
- **Menus:** keyboard focus rings and a logical tab order in every menu and dialog. Tab stays inside the screen
  or dialog that is open.
- **Any screen:** the menus fit phones and tablets in portrait or landscape, from 390 px wide to ultrawide.
  On a phone, menu controls are at least 44 px tall.
- **Interface layout** (*Settings › Graphics*): *Modern* (the build bar at the bottom) or *Classic* (Red
  Alert's sidebar on the right). Classic needs a landscape window at least 500 px tall where the sidebar
  takes at most 40% of the width (800×600 and up); on a smaller, narrower or portrait window the battle
  shows Modern and switches back when the window grows again (the setting is kept).
- **Performance overlay:** the FPS and renderer stats chip is off by default (*Settings › Graphics*).
- **Reduce motion:** calmer animations, and no camera shake or slow motion.
- **Copy diagnostics** (in Settings and on the crash screen): a plain-text report to paste into a bug
  report. It contains no personal data and is never sent anywhere.
- **Language:** English, Russian, German, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French or Simplified Chinese, in
  *Settings › Gameplay › Language*. *Auto* follows the browser. Change it from the main menu (it is locked
  during a battle). Everything is translated, missions and the map editor included. The announcer and the
  units speak your language when your browser has a voice for it; otherwise they speak English and the
  subtitles are in your language. (Some browser voices are online: they receive only the game's fixed lines,
  such as "Unit ready.")

## Maps, editor and mods

- **Maps.**
  - Nine hand-designed symmetric maps for 2–4 players.
  - Generated maps have shareable map codes.
- **Editor.** Open it from the main menu.
  - Paint terrain, height, forests, ore, bridges and start positions in 2D or live 3D, with
    symmetry helpers and undo.
  - Fairness and reachability checks, and one-click playtests.
  - A mission layer for forces, objectives and triggers.
  - Exports `.rhmap.json`.
- **Mods.** A `.rhmod.json` file overrides or clones unit, weapon and warhead values.
  - Mods are validated on import.
  - Saves and replays record them.
  - Examples are in [`public/mods/`](../public/mods/).

## Sharing

- **Share links.** **Copy link** in the editor, the scenario challenge list and the Replays list.
  - The map, challenge or short replay travels inside the link, after the `#`. Browsers never send that
    part to a server, and nothing is uploaded.
  - Whoever opens the link sees what it holds and chooses to import or play it.
  - Content too big for a link (long replays, large challenge snapshots) downloads as a file instead.
- **Highlight clips.** **Clip** next to a highlight on the results screen, or the camera button in the
  replay player.
  - The moment replays through a cinematic camera and is recorded with its sound: about 18 seconds of WebM
    video (MP4 in Safari).
  - Download it, or share it from a phone.
- **Trailer.** Open the game with `?trailer=1` for an ~85-second cut of a staged battle, which can be
  recorded too.

## Faithful to Red Alert

In 2025 EA released the original source code under the GPL
([electronicarts/CnC_Red_Alert](https://github.com/electronicarts/CnC_Red_Alert)). Red Horizon is
a clean-room engine that uses it as its rules reference:
- unit, weapon and warhead values come from `RULES.INI` ([`src/data/rules.ts`](../src/data/rules.ts));
- damage uses the original `Modify_Damage` formula ([`src/sim/combat.ts`](../src/sim/combat.ts));
- timing, speed and range conversions follow `DEFINES.H`.

A few values deliberately differ from the original. Each fixes an outlier found in cost-equal
simulation tests:

| Change | Original | Red Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Flame Tower range | 4 | 5 |
| Field Medic cost | $800 | $500 |
| Attack Dog health | 12 | 25, slightly faster |
| Turret health | 400 | 500 |
| Mammoth Tank cost | $1700 | $1900 |
